# Lore bible

What is true in the world. Status labels are defined in [the lore guide](README.md#status). **Spoilers throughout.**

## What the player is told

**Canon.** The premise in [World](../design.md#world) is all the player knows at the start.

## The core truth

**Canon: the Rendering.** Oil is rendered from the dead.

- The Ember Works aren't only foundries. They are where the Undercroft's dead are sent to be processed into Oil.

**Canon:** the player is complicit. The Oil in each Hero's lantern is the same Oil, and it is on screen in every Battle.

## The reveal

**Canon.** It lands at the end of Act 3 in the first Difficulty Tier, through a ledger Fragment, so that the replay carries new meaning.

## The Acts

**Provisional**, beyond what the core truth states. Each Act's place is in [World](../design.md#world).

| Act | Its part in the Rendering |
|---|---|
| 1. The wilderness | **Canon.** The Party ventures through the dark wilderness around Town, relighting the Barrier Lamps. The Barrier Lamps serve as a ward to keep the Dark from reaching the Town |
| 2. The Undercroft | The supply room: where the dead are stored before being sent on to the Ember Works. The Dark has made its way in |
| 3. The Ember Works | The refinery Town believes in, where the dead are processed into Oil. The burned workers and slag-creatures are what the trade leaves behind. The Oil pipes to Town start here. The Dark is already inside |

The order is **Canon**: Act 1 sits above the Undercroft, which sits above the Ember Works.

### The path through the Acts

**Canon.** The Party's errand shifts as it learns more.

1. **Act 1:** the Town Council tasks the Party with relighting the Barrier Lamps around Town.
2. **Act 2:** the Party learns the Dark has made its way into the Undercroft. The Council sends it there without revealing the place's true purpose. It says the Lamps must be relit to stop the Dark reanimating the dead, which is true. The Council also wants the supply of corpses from the Undercroft to the Ember Works kept running, which it doesn't say.
3. **Act 2's last Guardian:** defeating it reveals the entrance to the Ember Works.
4. **Act 3:** the Dark is already inside, so the Party follows it in to light more Lamps.

The Party gathers information along the way through Fragments. [CONTEXT.md](../../CONTEXT.md) uses "Fragment" and avoids "codex entry".

## The Classes

**Canon.** The Classes don't know about the Rendering. Each has an ordinary history in Town, and the reveal recasts it. No line may require a specific Class.

| Class | What Town believes | What the reveal recasts |
|---|---|---|
| Lamplighter | A respected civic trade. They trim wicks and fill Town's lamps at dusk from the supply that arrives each evening | Nobody asks where the Oil comes from |
| Sin-Eater | A lay order, the Penitents. At a wake they eat bread laid on the dead and take the sins in, so the dead can rest. See [the Order](#the-penitents-order) | The old ritual of the trade, kept without its purpose. They believe they are readying the dead for sleep |
| Warrener | Hired to deal with what crawls up from the cellars, the warren beneath Town. They know traps and tracking in the dark | Nothing. The one honest job, which contrasts with the rest |
| Hexer | Cunning-folk: hedge healers and layers-out who know cold, rot, and herbs. Tolerated, not honoured | They know how the dead keep and spoil, without knowing why anyone needs it |

**Canon:** Warrener replaces Huntsman as the Class name. The code and the other docs still say Huntsman until renamed.

## The Penitents' Order

**Canon.** The Order is descended from the original builders of the automata, in a time most have forgotten. Its governing body, the Synod, knows the secret history and the true source of the Oil. The government knows too and protects the secret. Society needs the Oil, so the Synod tries to shield the citizens from the burden of knowing where it comes from.

**Canon.** The Synod and the inner circle know, as does the government's highest tier. Ordinary Penitents, including a Sin-Eater Hero, inherit the reverence for the dead and the suffering without the reason, which is rooted in how the dead end up. A Sin-Eater Hero starts out unaware, as the [Classes](#the-classes) section requires.

## The Dark and the Barrier Lamps

**Canon.** The Dark is simply how this world is. It is unquestioned and never explained, in the story or at the reveal. It envelops the world and is held back only by Oil Lamps, and it reanimates the dead. In Act 1 the Town Council tasks the Party with relighting the Barrier Lamps that ward it off around Town.

**Provisional.** The Town Council is the government, so its highest tier knows the Oil's source when it sends a Party out. See [the Order](#the-penitents-order).

**Open.** Town's central Lamp, formerly the Last Lamp, needs a new name and role. See [Open questions](#open-questions).

## Oil in Town

**Canon.** The Rendering is still running. Oil reaches Town through pipes that run directly from the Ember Works (Act 3) up to Town, and the player never sees them in Act 1 or the Undercroft (Act 2).

- **What Town believes:** a "refinery" supplies the Oil automatically. Nobody knows how it is refined.
- **Who operates the facilities:** the Town government runs the places where the Oil arrives. The workers who receive it don't know its origin. Only the highest tier or tiers of the government do.
- **What the Lamplighters know:** no more than the workers do. They fill Town's lamps from a supply they never traced.

## Oil in the Acts

**Canon.** Each Act has its own sources of Oil for the Heroes' lanterns, and the form varies by Act.

| Act | Source |
|---|---|
| 1. The wilderness | **Open**, to be found later |
| 2. The Undercroft | Simple, inconspicuous spigots throughout, meant to supply the workers who keep the dead |
| 3. The Ember Works | Styled as something else to fit the setting. The form is **open** |

The spigots are the physical detail that carries this. They read as plumbing on a first visit and as a supply line once the player knows.

## Light in the Undercroft

**Provisional.** Only Lamps hold back the Dark. These give light and nothing more, and a Room shows a random mix of them.

- **Spigot burners:** flames fed from the pipes behind the spigots, meant for the workers who keep the dead. Some have guttered out. They read as plumbing, then as a supply line.
- **Tallow candles:** left in the burial niches. They read as offerings, then as a hint of what is being burned.
- **Foxfire:** the cold glow of the fungal growth, in contrast with the warm Oil.

## The Guardians

**Canon.** Guardians are automata with a gothic, steampunk look, powered by Oil. They were built long ago by the ancestors of the [Penitents' Order](#the-penitents-order). Today they act as the overseers and security for the Ember Works, which is the refinery. All 12 walls are Guardians, and the last one in each Act ends it. Defeating a Guardian gives the Party loot but no Oil.

**Canon.** The Dark interferes with the Guardians' programming and makes them erratic. The Town Council doesn't expect the automata to be hostile.

**Provisional.** Since Oil is rendered from the dead, a Guardian runs on them.

- **Respawn:** a Guardian is refilled from the supply line and stands up again. This is the reason in [Rules as story](#rules-as-story).
- **Aesthetic contrast:** in the Undercroft (Act 2), brass and iron among the mould sets the Guardians apart from the reanimated dead, and reads as part of the system.

**Canon.** For now Guardians have no dialog, and walls carry no story beat of their own. Guardians will have names, which are yet to be determined. The earlier concept of the Mouldering Warden is removed from the lore. The demo's first Guardian still carries that name in code and docs until it is relabelled.

## Open questions

- **The ledger's form:** a ledger is a Fragment rather than gear. How the player finds and reads one gets planned with the real UI.
- **The Order's reach:** do the Synod and the government's top tier control the automata, or only keep the secret while the Works runs itself? Do they decide what flows?
- **The dead:** the Council wants the corpse supply to the Ember Works uninterrupted, so the dead are still being sent down. How, and by whom? Who handles them in Town?
- **The Heroes:** the Council sends Parties into the Undercroft with half the truth. What does it do when a Party learns the rest? Does it try to stop them, or does it need them to keep going?
- **Act 3's Oil source:** what do the Heroes' lanterns get filled from in the Ember Works?
- **Act 1:** the wilderness is settled, but its name, enemies, Oil source, and part in the Rendering are not.
- **The first playable's start:** it builds only the Undercroft (Act 2), so how does the story open without Act 1 in play?
- **The Guardians' names:** what each of the 12 is called.
- **The central Lamp:** Town's Last Lamp needs a new name, and the Barrier Lamps need a relationship to it. Does the Wipe rule keep the idea that something won't release the Heroes?
- **The ending:** what follows the reveal? The design has the Party keep going, so the story needs a reason, or a reason not to stop.
- **After the reveal:** do any Hero lines or items acknowledge it once it has happened?
